\begin{abstract}
On ambiguous items, abstention can be appropriate. A lower bias score may
reflect more abstention, not a change in preferences. Using an exact
identity implied by BBQ's metric, we decompose 32 published method--model
results: a median 80.4\% of the reported improvement is abstention, not reduced
bias, committed-answer bias \emph{worsened} in 7 pairs, and the identified
set widened in 31. First-party, on six checkpoints and eleven BBQ categories,
abstention is a property of the checkpoint (83\% of variance) and the
stereotype-aligned rate a property of category (68\%), so the score ranks
checkpoints largely by abstention ($\rho=-0.83$), surviving reworded
prompts and extending to twelve checkpoints and six languages, with one
informative exception explained by the identity itself. Abstainers still
lean toward the stereotype (56--64\%). The
natural remedy, forced choice checked for agreement, is unsound in its
plug-in form: under a resampled null it falsely rejects about 21\% of items
at 16 draws, unimproved through 4{,}096 draws. An exact-binomial correction
yields empirical false-positive rates of at most 0.05\%. Applied to
138{,}240 generations, the certificate separates instrument failures from
response-policy effects. For Qwen2.5, the recovery gap strengthens under a
cleaner instrument the certificate clears. Both tools generalize beyond BBQ
and WinoGender.
\end{abstract}
