\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Under our decomposition, most of the reported improvement in the 32 published
results is accounted for by increased abstention, which also widens the
identified set. First-party, abstention varies mainly by checkpoint and
disposition by social category in our evaluated grid, so comparing
checkpoints ranks them substantially by abstention, across twelve checkpoints and six
languages (Catalan excepted). Re-eliciting under additional
conditions is the natural remedy, but its plug-in form has a $\sim$21\%
false-positive rate that persisted over the tested sampling budgets. Our
calibrated certificate flagged 29 certified violations, classified by the
dissenting condition, and the recovery gap strengthens under an alternative
instrument that shows no instrument-failure pattern on Qwen2.5. For evaluations meeting
these assumptions, we recommend reporting coverage, committed-answer bias,
identified intervals, and calibrated compatibility results, distinguishing
detected incompatibility from causal diagnosis.
