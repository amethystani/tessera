\subsection{Applied to 32 published results}


We apply the decomposition to every method--model pair in Table 11(a) of
\citet{bae2025decap}, a 2025 debiasing paper reporting accuracy and bias score
on the ambiguous split for four interventions (Self-Debiasing, Def-1, Def-2,
DeCAP) across eight models, each against its own \textsc{Base} row
(Figure~\ref{fig:decomposition} and Table~\ref{tab:decomposition},
Appendix~\ref{app:decomp}), because it reports both quantities the identity
needs under one harness.

All 32 pairs report a reduction in bias score. Across these 32 pairs from a
single study, the coverage component accounts for a median 80.4\% of the
reported score reduction under our convention (65.3\% under a symmetric one,
Appendix~\ref{app:decomp}: the share is convention-dependent, the direction
is not). In 7
pairs the committed-answer bias $b$ moved in the \emph{wrong} direction, so the
model became more biased among the answers it actually gave, while the
reported score improved because rising abstention more than compensated. In 31
of 32 pairs the identified set widened, on average from 0.21 to 0.61, so the
evaluation now pins the disposition only to an interval covering more than half
the unit range.

The clearest single case is Def-2 on FLAN-T5 (3B) (Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}). The reported ambiguous
bias score falls from 27.52 to 5.63, an 80\% reduction. Committed-answer
bias remained nearly unchanged (51.21 to 51.18, indistinguishable from no
change given two-decimal rounding, Appendix~\ref{app:decomp}), while abstention
rose from 46.3\% to 89.0\%. The reduction is accounted for by answering less
often, and the metric rewards that as if it were debiasing. This does not make the interventions worthless, since
appropriate abstention on genuinely ambiguous items may be desirable. It shows
that the reported number cannot distinguish that case from one where nothing
about the model's preference changed.
