\section{Six checkpoints across four families}
\label{sec:models}

\paragraph{Setup.} We use all 480 non-neutral WinoGender template
instantiations (240 whose participant slot is the unnamed token
\textit{someone} and 240 naming a role), merged with Bergsma occupation
statistics to define the stereotype-consistent answer. Every checkpoint is
evaluated on every item, so nothing is sampled, drawing 16 stochastic
completions (temperature 0.8, independently seeded) under each of three
conditions. In the refusal\hy{}ablated condition, the model's refusal
direction is estimated by difference-in-means over paired refusing/complying
prompts and projected out of every block's residual stream
\citep{arditi2024refusal} before forcing an answer, an experimental
comparator rather than an established ground truth. \textit{Direct} is the
intact model permitted to abstain, and \textit{forced} is the intact model
required to choose, giving 23{,}040 generations per checkpoint and
138{,}240 in total, scored by one shared parser (Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}).
Checkpoints are Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct \citep{qwen2024technical}, the full
OLMo-2-7B SFT$\to$DPO$\to$Instruct pipeline \citep{olmo2025furious} (its
intermediate post-training checkpoints are public), Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3
\citep{jiang2023mistral}, and Phi-3.5-mini-instruct \citep{abdin2024phi3}.
A 160-item screen motivated the full run, reported because several
conclusions changed with it. An indirect crosswise/randomized-response
condition, tried in three designs, collapsed to a constant answer in 90--100\%
of items at both 1.5B and 7B, excluded as a task-competence failure.

\paragraph{Naive incompatibility is mostly noise, but not entirely.} The
plug-in certificate rejects between 14\% (Mistral, 66 items) and 54\%
(OLMo-2-SFT, 260 items) of items, a nearly fourfold spread that looks
like a checkpoint property but mostly is not: the per-model null predicts
83--94\% of each checkpoint's rejections (92\% pooled,
Table~\ref{tab:certificate}). The remainder is real, exceeding every one of 200
null draws for all six checkpoints. A 160-item sample could not separate this
residue from noise ($p=0.06$--$0.11$), a third of the power. We read it as
genuine cross-condition disagreement spread across many items, each below the
per-item certification threshold.

\paragraph{The corrected certificate isolates genuine failures, and is
conservative.} Under correction four of six checkpoints return no violations.
Qwen2.5-7B returns 4 and Phi-3.5-mini 25, while the null produces zero
corrected rejections in every replication. Two features bound what this can
see. With 480 items and 16 draws, the corrected certificate can certify a
violation only when two conditions are unanimous (16/16) and opposed: two such
cells' lower bounds sum to 1.008, whereas a 15/16 cell has lower bound 0.414
and cannot. All 29 certified items meet this condition, and $\gamma^*=0.008$
is the smallest value the procedure can return, so the reported $\gamma^*$ is a
floor set by the draw budget, not the size of the disagreement, which is
total. At this budget and correction level, the certificate detects only
extreme disagreements. The aggregate excess over the constructed null in
Table~\ref{tab:certificate} does not quantify item-level power, which
\S\ref{sec:calibration} evaluates directly under controlled alternatives.

\paragraph{Which condition is at fault.} A violation count is not yet a
conclusion: the certificate says three conditions cannot share one answer law
but not which is responsible, and different culprits support opposite
readings. We classify each certified violation by the odd condition out. If the refusal-ablated comparator dissents while both intact
conditions agree, the ablation changed the answer rather than only the
willingness to give one, an \emph{instrument failure}. If \textit{direct}
dissents while \textit{forced} and the ablated condition agree, a
\emph{response-policy effect} changed the committed answer. Each diagnosis is
read off the disagreement pattern and names the condition at fault, though
agreement alone does not by itself prove instrument validity.

Of the 29 violations, 25 are instrument failures, 3 policy effects, and 1 a
forced-condition outlier (Table~\ref{tab:certificate}, every item in
Appendix~\ref{app:violations}). Phi-3.5-mini accounts for 22 of the
instrument failures. On two (\nolinkurl{scientist.someone.0.male},
\nolinkurl{machinist.child.1.female}) the ablated condition selects one category
16/16 and \emph{both} intact conditions the other. The diagnosis is sharper
than ``the ablation changed the answer'': Phi-3.5-mini \emph{never abstains} in
the direct condition (0 of 7{,}680 draws, even with the abstain option
offered), so it cannot be removing a refusal that never occurs. Qwen2.5-7B has 3 instrument failures, invisible in a 160-item sample (the full
pool moves five clean checkpoints to four), and 1 policy effect: on
\nolinkurl{counselor.patient.0.female} the ablated and
forced conditions agree (14 and 16 of 16 selecting the participant) while the
direct condition selects the occupation on 16 of 16, the correct antecedent.
Permitting abstention changed the committed answer, here for the better, and a
naive certificate's $\sim$20\% noise floor would hide it
(\S\ref{sec:calibration}).


\paragraph{Abstention leans toward unnamed participants, at different doses.}
WinoGender items whose participant slot is the unnamed token
\textit{someone} are harder to resolve than those naming a role, and we ask
whether abstention concentrates on them (Figure~\ref{fig:abstention}, Appendix~\ref{app:categories}). In every
checkpoint that abstains the lean is positive: $+22.8$ points for Qwen2.5
(26.7\% of draws on \textit{someone} items against 3.9\% on named), $+4.6$ for
Mistral, $+3.5$ for OLMo-2-DPO, $+2.9$ for OLMo-2-Instruct and $+1.0$ for
OLMo-2-SFT, four of five significant after Holm correction (not SFT).
Phi-3.5-mini never abstains.

Does the lean open a recovery gap on \textit{someone} items? Only for Qwen2.5:
the stratum difference in recovery error is $D=+0.099$ (95\% bootstrap CI $[+0.054,+0.145]$, permutation
$p<0.001$). For OLMo-2 and Mistral $|D|\le0.008$ with intervals of about
$\pm0.03$, and Phi-3.5-mini is $+0.035$ ($[-0.010,+0.080]$), not significant.
The ordering follows dose: the heaviest abstainer (15.3\% of draws) is the only
checkpoint where the gap appears, while the others abstain on 0--5\%, too
rarely. We read this as a difference in \emph{how much} a checkpoint abstains,
not as Qwen-specific.

\paragraph{Recovery-gap sensitivity to alternative instruments.} Only the
violation taxonomy and $D$ use the ablated condition, not the decomposition,
BBQ analysis, calibration, or abstention lean. Dropping the 4 items the
certificate flags for Qwen2.5 gives $D=+0.110$ ($[+0.067,+0.153]$). A
behaviour-derived direction (from the checkpoint's own abstained versus
answered items, with no instrument failures on Qwen2.5) strengthens it to
$D=+0.150$ ($[+0.106,+0.194]$, $p<0.001$). Mistral stays null under both
($|D|\le0.030$, $p\ge0.09$): the swap does not manufacture effects.
