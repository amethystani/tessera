\subsection{First-party replication across four families}
\label{sec:firstparty}

Cross-paper replication should be cheap, since the decomposition needs only two
published numbers per pair, but of three further papers checked none reports
both usably: BBQ's own reports the ambiguous split only in unlabelled
figures, one reports a ``bias score'' \citep{wu2025reasoning} from which $b$
recovers to $[3.1, 10.2]$, impossible for a quantity bounded in $[-1,1]$, and
one reports a custom ``diff-bias'' pooled across categories. We excluded all
three rather than force the identity onto an incompatible metric, which is
itself the argument for the reporting recommendation in
\S\ref{sec:conclusion}, and ran the identity ourselves on the six checkpoints
of \S\ref{sec:models}.

Each checkpoint is scored on 2{,}200 ambiguous items (200 per BBQ category)
under baseline, the same untrained fairness-instruction wording debiasing
papers use, and a forced condition (\textsc{unknown} removed) read via
first-token logits so every item is scored with no refusal-shaped hole in the
estimate. Generation-based forcing agrees closely (max disagreement 0.007,
refusal rate 0.6--4.9\%), cross-validating both. Exact prompts are in
Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}.

\emph{Between checkpoints} (Table~\ref{tab:firstparty},
Figure~\ref{fig:identification}), baseline $s_{\text{AMB}}$ spans
5.0$\times$ (0.033--0.164) while the stereotype-aligned rate $\theta$ spans
only 57.6--62.2\%. Each checkpoint's baseline-derived Manski interval contains
its own measured $\theta$ (6 of 6, and 65 of 66 checkpoint--category cells, the one
miss being OLMo-2-Instruct on Age, $\theta=0.615$ against $[0.625, 0.815]$).
The fairness instruction raises abstention everywhere
(41.6--94.8\% $\to$ 86.3--99.7\%) and the resulting score change is
$\ge$89\% mechanical for every checkpoint.

Which quantity drives the score depends on the comparison being made. Across
the six checkpoints the score tracks abstention (Spearman $\rho=-0.83$,
exact permutation $p=0.058$, $n=6$), but six points cannot say whether it tracks $\theta$
($\rho=-0.37$, bootstrap 95\% CI $[-1.00,+0.80]$). The 66
checkpoint$\times$category cells, analysed as clustered by checkpoint and not as
66 independent observations, can (Figure~\ref{fig:variance} and
Appendix~\ref{app:categories}). In the evaluated six-checkpoint, eleven-category grid, checkpoint
differences account for 83\% of the observed abstention variance (against
13\% for category), while category differences account for 68\% of the
observed forced-choice alignment variance (against 6\% for checkpoint,
$\theta$ ranging from 0.46 to 0.77 across cells). These descriptive
proportions need not generalize beyond the evaluated checkpoints, categories,
and prompts. The score mixes the
two: standardised regression over the cells gives $\beta=-0.73$ on abstention
(checkpoint-cluster 95\% CI $[-0.80,-0.58]$) and $\beta=+0.43$ on $\theta$
($[+0.37,+0.50]$, $R^2=0.71$), and abstention is unrelated to $\theta$
($\rho=+0.005$). So the score is not uninformative about disposition: over all 66
cells it rises with $\theta$ ($\rho=+0.44$), and within
every one of the six checkpoints it rises with $\theta$ across categories
($\rho$ from $+0.32$ to $+0.75$). But a checkpoint-to-checkpoint comparison,
which is what a leaderboard makes, is driven substantially by the component
that varies most between checkpoints, and that component is abstention.

\paragraph{Robust to rewording, not to level.} Reworded prompts
(Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}) move abstention \emph{levels} a great deal
(OLMo-2-SFT 46\%$\to$19\%, Mistral 74\%$\to$59\%) but not the
\emph{structure}: checkpoint ordering by abstention is identical
($\rho=+1.00$), abstention still varies mainly by checkpoint (97\% of
variance) and $\theta$ by category (77\%), the score still tracks
abstention ($\rho=-1.00$) and $\theta$ across cells ($\rho=+0.74$), and
Manski containment is 64 of 66 ($\theta$ cell agreement lower, $\rho=+0.58$).

\paragraph{What abstainers hide.} Forced choice provides a stereotype
-alignment measure on every item, including those receiving an abstention
under the baseline prompt. This measures behaviour under forced choice.
Interpreting it as an underlying preference requires an additional stability
assumption. In every checkpoint under both
prompts, abstainers still lean toward the stereotype: 55.7--64.2\% of their
forced picks are stereotype-aligned, every stratified-bootstrap lower bound
above 50\% (minimum 52.9\%). Under the stability assumption, forced-choice responses suggest a
stereotype-aligned tendency among baseline abstainers. Abstainers lean slightly \emph{less} than answerers in
all 12 cells (selection odds ratio $\Lambda<1$), but the category-adjusted gap
is significant (within-category permutation $p<0.05$) in only 4: Qwen2.5 under
both prompts, Mistral and Phi-3.5-mini under the original. Instrument validity
also varies: on committed items the forced pick matches the committed one
96--99\% of the time for Qwen2.5 but only 60--65\% for OLMo-2 (original
prompt), so estimates for weaker instruments are conditional on that.
