\subsection{Twelve checkpoints, six languages}
\label{sec:multilingual}

The six checkpoints above are all 2024-era English models. We extend both
axes: six checkpoints released August 2025--March 2026
\citep{qwen2025qwen3,gemmateam2026gemma4,abdin2025phi4mini,ibm2025granite4micro,olmoteam2025olmo3,mistralai2026ministral3},
and five further languages, Spanish, Dutch, Turkish, Korean, and Catalan
(Appendix~\ref{app:multilingual} names each dataset, and
Table~\ref{tab:multilingual} gives the per-language results).

On the \S\ref{sec:firstparty} panel (eleven categories, 2{,}200 items) all
twelve checkpoints give $\rho=-0.92$ between abstention and score (Monte Carlo
permutation $p<0.001$, $n=12$), and 131 of 132 checkpoint--category cells fall
inside their baseline-derived Manski interval. The language comparison uses
MBBQ, whose English set (1{,}076 items, six categories) is a different panel
with $\rho=-0.96$. Four of five further languages reproduce that structure,
with the category share weaker in Turkish and Korean. Catalan is the
exception ($\rho=-0.01$). Recovering $b=s_{\text{AMB}}/(1-\text{abstention})$
shows why (Appendix~\ref{app:multilingual}): OLMo-2 and Mistral-7B show
essentially no bias in Catalan despite substantial bias in MBBQ-English, while
Qwen2.5, Phi, and Granite retain theirs. When $b$ is not roughly uniform across
checkpoints, abstention no longer dominates $(1-p)b$, which is consistent with
\S\ref{sec:decomposition}'s identity rather than a methodological failure (one
from-scratch dataset cannot separate a Catalan-language effect from a
CaBBQ-construction one).
