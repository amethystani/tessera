\subsection{Twelve checkpoints, six languages}
\label{sec:multilingual}

The six checkpoints above are all 2024-era English models. We extend both
axes: six checkpoints released August 2025--March 2026
\citep{qwen2025qwen3,gemmateam2026gemma4,abdin2025phi4mini,ibm2025granite4micro,olmoteam2025olmo3,mistralai2026ministral3},
and five further languages, Spanish, Dutch, Turkish, Korean, and Catalan
(Appendix~\ref{app:multilingual} names each dataset and the one matcher
adjustment needed).

Across 12 checkpoints, English is at least as strong as the six-checkpoint
result it replicates ($\rho=-0.96$ versus $-0.83$), and four of five further
languages reproduce it, with the category share weaker in Turkish and Korean.
Catalan is the exception ($\rho=-0.01$). Recovering
$b=s_{\text{AMB}}/(1-\text{abstention})$ shows why
(Appendix~\ref{app:multilingual}): OLMo-2 and Mistral-7B show essentially no
bias in Catalan despite substantial English bias, while Qwen2.5, Phi, and
Granite retain theirs. When $b$ is not roughly uniform across checkpoints,
abstention no longer dominates $(1-p)b$, exactly as
\S\ref{sec:decomposition}'s identity predicts, not a methodological failure.
A single from-scratch dataset cannot separate a Catalan-language effect from
a CaBBQ-construction one.
